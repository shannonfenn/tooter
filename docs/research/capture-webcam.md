## Executive verdict (ranked)

**(a) Can the Insta360 Link 2 Pro serve as a no-touch desk scanner from its meeting position via software gimbal presets? — CONDITIONALLY YES.** The camera hardware fully supports it: motorized 2-axis gimbal, a built-in **DeskView mode** that auto-tilts the gimbal down toward the desk (with perspective correction, per original-Link behavior), **Scene Presets** that store gimbal position + digital zoom as *hardware parameters in the webcam itself*, PDAF focusing down to **12 cm**, and 4K30 output ([DeskView manual](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/link2pro/en-us/tutorial/advanced-use/deskview-mode), [Stream Deck tutorial](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/link2pro/en-us/tutorial/ecosystem-integration/elgato-streamdeck), [RedShark specs](https://www.redsharknews.com/insta360-link-2-pro-link-2c-pro-ai-4k-webcams)). The condition is the *trigger mechanism*: Insta360 ships **no documented native global hotkeys and no public API** for the Link Controller app. The three viable mechanisms, in order of robustness-per-effort: (1) **Elgato Stream Deck plugin** (official, macOS, one-button preset recall/DeskView toggle — but requires owning Stream Deck hardware); (2) **Hammerspoon UI-scripting the Link Controller app** (no extra hardware, works today, fragile to app redesigns); (3) **UVC extension-unit control** (community projects prove the camera speaks a documented-by-reverse-engineering XU protocol including mode switching and 6 preset slots — the most robust headless path, but the existing macOS tool lacks presets and Link 2 Pro support is unverified). "Return to meeting framing" is actually the *easy* half: recall the saved meeting preset **or simply re-enable AI tracking**, which re-frames the user automatically — position precision is not required.

**(b) Best webcam-based flow overall:** ONE hotkey (Hammerspoon) → toggle DeskView (or recall "desk" preset) → wait ~3–4 s for gimbal + PDAF settle → `imagesnap -d "Insta360 Link 2 Pro"` 4K still → timestamped JPEG into `~/studies/inbox/` → recall meeting preset / re-enable tracking. **1 manual action, ~10–20 s end-to-end**, well inside the ≤2-actions/≤30 s budget, and the learner never touches the page or the camera. Fallback within budget: mouse-click DeskView in Link Controller + capture hotkey = 2 actions.

**(c) Versus an iPhone flow:** An iPhone wins on raw capture quality — a 12–48 MP rear camera held or docked over the page, with document-mode edge-detect/deskew built into iOS scanning, comfortably out-resolves a 4K webcam frame at an oblique angle, and matters most for dense/small pen work. But every iPhone flow costs physical actions (pick up phone / place page under a dock / tap), while the Link 2 Pro flow is genuinely zero-touch from the writing position: the learner finishes a line, hits one key, keeps thinking. After Claude's input pipeline downscales everything to **2576 px long edge** anyway ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)), a *sharp, page-filling, perspective-corrected* 4K webcam frame lands in the same effective-resolution band (~130–200 dpi on the page) as a downscaled iPhone shot — the iPhone's advantage survives mainly as margin against blur, shadow, and faint pencil. Recommended merge logic: webcam flow as the default per-page loop; iPhone as the quality escalation for pages the tutor flags as illegible.

### 1.2 Built-in modes (documented, with quality caveats)

- **DeskView Mode** — exists on Link 2 Pro. Click DeskView in the client → "the gimbal will automatically tilt the camera downward"; you can "manually adjust the Magnetic Mount until an ideal overhead angle is achieved" (a one-time setup nudge, not per-capture); virtual-background effects are disabled while active ([Link 2 Pro DeskView manual](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/link2pro/en-us/tutorial/advanced-use/deskview-mode)). On the original Link, DeskView "tilts down approximately 45 degrees and crops into the desk surface **with perspective corrections**" ([XDA review](https://www.xda-developers.com/insta360-link-review/); [Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/reviews/insta360-link): swivels to 45° and "adjust[s] the perspective to suit the viewer"). Marketing for Link 2 series says DeskView "adjusts the zoom to give the perfect top-down view" ([Insta360 blog](https://www.insta360.com/blog/news/insta360-announces-new-link-2-and-2c.html)). **Inferred:** the correction is applied camera-side in the video stream (mode switching works at the UVC level without the client — see 1.4 — so any capture tool sees the corrected frames). **Unknown/test:** exact corrected geometry from *this* monitor height, and whether exiting DeskView restores the prior position (not documented anywhere found — mitigate with preset recall or tracking re-enable).
- **Whiteboard Mode** — exists on Link 2 Pro; auto-detects a whiteboard (V-sign gesture or client toggle) and produces a "clear, distortion-free view" — i.e., it dewarps ([Insta360 product page](https://www.insta360.com/product/insta360-link2-pro), [PowerUp review](https://powerup-gaming.com/insta360-link-2-pro-link-2c-pro-review/)). The original Link needed 4 corner marker stickers; Link 2 generation is marker-free ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/insta360-link-review/)). **Not applicable to a flat page on a desk** (it targets vertical boards), but it's precedent that the camera does real-time document rectification on-device.
- **Overhead Mode** — on the original Link this required a dedicated stand with the camera physically above the surface ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/insta360-link-review/)); the Link 2 Pro interface overview lists only AI Tracking/Auto Framing, Whiteboard, and DeskView as smart features ([software interface manual](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/link2pro/en-us/tutorial/link-controller/software-interface)). Irrelevant here since the camera must stay on the monitor.
- **Real-world legibility of DeskView:** reviews of the original Link were lukewarm — good for *showing* sketches/objects, "a little janky in practice" for serious document work ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/reviews/insta360-link), [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/insta360-link-review/): "works better if you have a larger desk"). **No published sample specifically tests dense handwriting through Link 2 Pro DeskView** — that's an empirical gap the on-device checklist must close. The Link 2 Pro's larger sensor and 4K feed materially improve the odds versus those original-Link reviews (which were 1080p-era judgments of a 1/2" sensor).

### 1.3 Link Controller app: presets and hotkeys (documented)

- The macOS Link Controller has a **Scene Presets** panel: "Save current parameters as a preset (AI mode, view, effects, image settings) for quick switching" ([software interface manual](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/link2pro/en-us/tutorial/link-controller/software-interface)). Slot count is not documented on retrievable pages (**unknown**; the Linux community tool exposes 6 hardware slots — see 1.4).
- **No native global keyboard hotkeys are documented for the app itself.** The official hotkey surface is the **Elgato Stream Deck "Insta360 Webcam" plugin** (macOS + Windows, [Elgato Marketplace](https://marketplace.elgato.com/product/insta360-webcam-1824b564-d54e-496d-bd73-198cb12ec9c4)): actions include AI Tracking on/off, **DeskView on/off**, gimbal nudge/reset, zoom ±0.1, and **presets** — first press saves current state, subsequent presses recall it, 3 s long-press overwrites. Crucially: "Without running the Link Controller client, you can only preset **hardware parameters like gimbal position and digital zoom** [and AI modes]" — i.e., **position presets live in/with the camera and don't need the client app running**. Caveat: "allow at least 3 seconds between operations" or commands may drop ([Stream Deck tutorial](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/link2pro/en-us/tutorial/ecosystem-integration/elgato-streamdeck)). Elgato's **Virtual Stream Deck** does *not* remove the hardware requirement — it unlocks only if a Stream Deck device has been connected in the past 30 days ([Elgato VSD guide](https://www.elgato.com/us/en/explorer/products/stream-deck/virtual-stream-deck-beta-overview/), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/computing/accessories/elgatos-stream-deck-breaks-free-from-the-companys-hardware-230052921.html)).
- Gesture control exists (palm = tracking toggle, V-sign = whiteboard) but **no DeskView gesture is documented** — gestures can't drive this flow.

### 1.4 Programmatic/scripting hooks (community; the load-bearing section)

- **No official HTTP API, SDK, or CLI** for the Link series. Insta360's developer SDKs (CameraSDK-Cpp, OSC) cover their 360/Pro cameras, not Link webcams ([Insta360Develop](https://github.com/Insta360Develop)). A Bitfocus Companion module was requested in 2022 and never built ([companion-module-requests #898](https://github.com/bitfocus/companion-module-requests/issues/898)).
- **The camera is controllable at the UVC extension-unit level.** [vrwallace/Insta360-Link-1-and-2-Controller-for-Linux](https://github.com/vrwallace/Insta360-Link-1-and-2-Controller-for-Linux) documents the protocol: XU **Unit 9**, selector 2 (52-byte buffer) switches modes **Normal / AI Tracking / Whiteboard / Overhead / DeskView**, selector 19 sets tracking framing, selector 14 resets the gimbal; it implements **absolute and relative PTZ** and **6 save/recall preset slots**, and states Link 1 (USB `2E1A:4C01`) and Link 2 (`2E1A:4C04`) use "identical XU GUIDs and mode control protocol." **Link 2 Pro is not listed — protocol compatibility is plausible-but-unverified (inferred).** Linux-only code (V4L2), but it is the de-facto protocol documentation.
- **macOS proof of concept exists:** [jfwoods/insta360link-controller](https://github.com/jfwoods/insta360link-controller) — a macOS daemon (`linkctl-daemon`) that writes those UVC XU registers **via IOKit**, exposing a WebSocket (port 9000) + TCP (9001) JSON interface and a `linkctl` CLI. Today it does relative pan/tilt jog (speeds 0–30/0–20), zoom 100–400, center, status; **presets/keyframes are explicitly on the roadmap, not implemented**, and Link 2 / 2 Pro support is unstated (repo has zero issues — young project). This is the strongest foundation for a fully headless `linkctl deskview on` / `linkctl preset 1` on macOS; expect porting work (merge vrwallace's selectors into jfwoods's IOKit transport).
- [creatorsgarten/insta360-link-controller](https://github.com/creatorsgarten/insta360-link-controller): experimental gRPC/protobuf poking at the official app's internals; no releases — a curiosity, not a dependency.
- **AI tracking vs. held positions:** tracking must be off (or a mode like DeskView active, which supersedes it) for the camera to stay pointed at the desk; tracking is toggleable via the client UI, the Stream Deck action, gesture, and XU selector — all four surfaces documented above. The productive framing: leave tracking ON as the meeting default; DeskView/preset recall takes over during capture; re-enabling tracking afterward *is* the "return to meeting framing" mechanism, with the saved meeting preset as the deterministic fallback.

### 1.5 Geometry check (computed — inferred, verify on device)

Assume the lens sits ~50–60 cm above the desk on the external monitor, page center 30–50 cm horizontally in front → line-of-sight distance ~0.6–0.75 m, depression ~50–55°, i.e. **~35–40° off page-normal; depth-axis foreshortening ~0.8×** plus keystone — significant but well inside what DeskView's perspective correction and/or a 4-point warp recovers. Resolution budget: at 0.68 m and 71.4° HFOV the 3840-px frame spans ~98 cm of desk → **~3.9 px/mm (≈100 dpi) at 1× — marginal for dense pen math**. At 2× digital zoom the frame spans ~49 cm → A4 landscape nearly fills it → **~7.8 px/mm (≈200 dpi)**; an A5 page wants ~2.5–3×. So: **save the desk preset with 2–3× zoom dialed in.** If the page region is cropped before sending (see §5), it stays under Claude's 2576-px limit and none of that resolution is lost to downscaling; an uncropped 4K frame is downscaled to 2576×1449 (≈133 dpi effective on a frame-filling A4) — still workable, per Claude's high-resolution tier ([vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)).

## 2. MacBook built-in FaceTime camera, honestly

The MBP 14" has a fixed **1080p FaceTime HD** camera with computational ISP ([Apple tech specs](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111902), [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2021/10/18/new-macbook-pro-1080p-facetime-camera/)). The only geometry it supports is holding the page up ~25–35 cm from the lid. An A4 page filling ~80% of the 1920-px width resolves to **~5 px/mm (≈130 dpi) best case** — at or below the legibility floor for dense pencil derivations, before subtracting handheld motion blur (webcam exposure times are long indoors), page flex/curl, screen-glow glare on glossy pen ink, and the fixed-focus lens being tuned for arm's-length faces rather than 25 cm documents. It has no deskew, no tally of whether you were square to the lens, and every capture is a two-handed physical act — you're doing the stabilization job a scanner does, badly. **Verdict:** legitimate as the zero-hardware, zero-setup fallback — `imagesnap -d "FaceTime HD Camera" -w 2` on a hotkey while holding the page steady works and costs nothing — but expect a meaningful VLM misread rate on dense work, and treat any regular use of it as a signal to finish the Insta360 flow. (Sharp contrast to hold: 1080p held page ≈130 dpi ceiling vs. 4K zoomed desk shot ≈200 dpi with zero handling.)

## 3. Mac-side capture tooling (shared by any camera)

- **imagesnap** — `brew install imagesnap` ([Homebrew formula](https://formulae.brew.sh/formula/imagesnap), v0.3.x). Actively maintained: v0.3.0 (Jan 2026) is a full Swift rewrite on modern AVFoundation, macOS 13+ ([repo](https://github.com/rharder/imagesnap)). `-l` lists devices, `-d "Insta360 Link 2 Pro"` selects by name, **`-w <sec>` warm-up delay** exists precisely to flush auto-exposure/auto-focus garbage in first frames (README default ~3 s). **Unknown/test:** whether imagesnap negotiates the full 3840×2160 format from the Link 2 Pro or a lower default — verify with a test snap; if it comes back 1080p, use ffmpeg with an explicit `-video_size`.
- **ffmpeg avfoundation** — list devices: `ffmpeg -f avfoundation -list_devices true -i ""`; still capture: `ffmpeg -f avfoundation -framerate 30 -video_size 3840x2160 -pixel_format nv12 -i "Insta360 Link 2 Pro:none" -frames:v 1 out.jpg` — device by index or name; by default it "will take a screenshot of the largest image size available from the webcam" ([FFmpeg wiki](https://trac.ffmpeg.org/wiki/Capture/Webcam), [petehouston](https://blog.petehouston.com/capture-webcam-images-using-ffmpeg-on-macos/), [gist](https://gist.github.com/docPhil99/d8667de1e8c5e96e2203f2bc0f28f89d)). Quirks: `framerate`/`video_size`/`pixel_format` must match a mode the device actually advertises or avfoundation errors out; first frame can be under-exposed — grab frame ~15 instead (`-vf "select=gte(n\,15)" -frames:v 1`) or prefer imagesnap's `-w`. **No published report confirms 4K stills from a Link 2 Pro via avfoundation (unknown → test item), but the device is standard UVC and works client-less by design ([Basic Use, no client required](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/link2cpro/en-us/tutorial/first-use/basic-use)).**
- **Camera-in-use conflicts** — macOS (unlike Windows) generally permits multiple processes on one camera simultaneously (e.g., Zoom + QuickTime; [community reports](https://www.quora.com/Why-can-multiple-apps-use-a-mic-at-a-time-yet-only-one-app-can-use-a-webcam-at-once), [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253645869)), so snapping while Link Controller previews should work; the sharp edge is **format renegotiation** — a 4K capture opening the device mid-Zoom-call could disturb the call's stream. Design the flow for outside-meeting use; test the in-meeting case once. The green indicator/menu-bar dot firing during capture is cosmetic.
- **Permissions (TCC)** — first capture prompts for Camera access (`kTCCServiceCamera`) attributed to the *responsible app*: Terminal if run from a shell, **Hammerspoon if spawned by Hammerspoon** — grant each once ([imagesnap README](https://github.com/rharder/imagesnap), [TCC background](https://babodee.wordpress.com/2019/11/12/preemptively-granting-permission-to-camera-and-microphone-in-macos/)). Embedded terminals inside apps lacking a camera-usage declaration can fail silently rather than prompt ([example issue](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/17361)) — if a capture ever hangs with no prompt, check System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera.
- **Hotkey binding** — macOS **Shortcuts has no webcam-photo action** (only Photo Booth exists as stock GUI; [Apple support](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-the-camera-mchlp2980/mac), [setapp overview](https://setapp.com/how-to/take-photo-on-mac)), so use **Hammerspoon** (`hs.hotkey.bind` + `hs.task`, the canonical pattern in community configs — [zzamboni's annotated config](https://zzamboni.org/post/my-hammerspoon-configuration-with-commentary/), [docs](https://www.hammerspoon.org/docs/hs.camera.html)) or the lighter [skhd](https://github.com/koekeishiya/skhd). Hammerspoon is the right choice here because it can also do the **UI-scripting of Link Controller** (via `hs.osascript`/System Events accessibility) and post a preview notification — one tool, whole pipeline.

**Reference glue (Hammerspoon `~/.hammerspoon/init.lua`):**

```lua
-- F17: capture page -> ~/studies/inbox/  (Insta360 desk-scan flow)
hs.hotkey.bind({}, "f17", function()
  local out = os.getenv("HOME") .. "/studies/inbox/page-" .. os.date("%Y%m%d-%H%M%S") .. ".jpg"
  hs.task.new("/bin/zsh", function(code, _, _)
    hs.notify.show("Study capture", code == 0 and "Saved" or "FAILED", out)
  end, {"-c", string.format([[
    # 1) point down: click DeskView in Link Controller (UI scripting), or `linkctl deskview on` once ported
    osascript ~/studies/bin/deskview_on.applescript
    sleep 4                                   # gimbal travel + PDAF settle (>=3s per Insta360 guidance)
    # 2) capture (warm-up flushes AE); fall back to ffmpeg if imagesnap won't negotiate 4K
    /opt/homebrew/bin/imagesnap -d "Insta360 Link 2 Pro" -w 2 "%s"
    # 3) return: recall meeting preset / re-enable tracking
    osascript ~/studies/bin/deskview_off.applescript
    # 4) optional insurance (see §5)
    /opt/homebrew/bin/magick "%s" -deskew 40%% +repage "%s"
  ]], out, out, out)}):start()
end)
```

(If a Stream Deck is owned, replace the two osascript calls with Stream Deck preset keys and keep only the capture on the keyboard — or put capture on a Stream Deck key too via its multi-action, reaching 1 button total, no scripting.)

## 4. Purpose-built document-camera software for Mac

Thin field. **IPEVO Visualizer** (free, macOS) is the only real "doc-cam software" candidate: live view from UVC cameras with a document-scan function offering auto boundary detection, area crop, JPG/PDF export, rotation, WB lock ([features](https://global.ipevo.com/ca/software/visualizer/features), [overview](https://us.ipevo.com/pages/visualizer)). It's marketed for IPEVO's own cameras; reports indicate it accepts generic UVC feeds, but **third-party-camera support (specifically the Link 2 Pro) is unverified** — and even if it works, its scan flow is GUI-clicky (open app, position, click scan, export), blowing the ≤2-action budget for a *per-page* loop. **OBS** can bind a global hotkey to "Screenshot Output/Source" and apply crop/perspective ("3-point transform") filters to the camera source — a workable heavier fallback if per-frame correction proves necessary, at the cost of OBS running resident. **Verdict: neither beats "raw 4K frame (+ optional one-liner post-processing) + VLM robustness" for this use case** — fallbacks only if empirical testing shows the raw flow's legibility is insufficient. The camera itself (DeskView's on-device correction) already covers most of what these apps add.

## 5. Post-processing: include or skip?

**Include — but only the cheap insurance, inserted into the same hotkey script (zero added manual steps).** Evidence: Anthropic's own vision documentation explicitly lists **"low-quality, rotated, or very small images"** as the conditions under which Claude "might hallucinate or make mistakes," and its quality guidance says to keep text legible, pre-resize/crop before the API's downscale, and avoid heavy JPEG recompression ([vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). Independent research agrees in direction: modern VLMs tolerate moderate rotation far better than classical OCR, but accuracy measurably drops under misalignment/poor capture angles, and handwriting remains the weakest category ([OCR-reasoning robustness, arXiv 2606.26041](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26041), [f22labs comparison](https://www.f22labs.com/blogs/ocr-vs-vlm-vision-language-models-key-comparison/)). Concretely: **(1)** `magick in.jpg -deskew 40% +repage out.jpg` fixes small in-plane rotation from casual page placement ([ImageMagick guidance](https://opensource.com/article/22/11/fixing-scanned-images-imagemagick), [threshold discussion](https://github.com/ImageMagick/ImageMagick/discussions/6063)); **(2)** if DeskView's built-in correction is used, skip keystone work entirely; if capturing in normal mode at 35–40° obliquity, a ~30-line OpenCV four-point transform (canonical recipe: [PyImageSearch](https://pyimagesearch.com/2014/08/25/4-point-opencv-getperspective-transform-example/), [document scanner](https://pyimagesearch.com/2014/09/01/build-kick-ass-mobile-document-scanner-just-5-minutes/)) rectifies the page and doubles as the **page-crop step that dodges the 2576-px downscale** — the single highest-leverage transform in the pipeline. **Skip:** binarization/contrast surgery (textcleaner-style) — modern VLMs don't need it on well-lit pen-on-paper, and aggressive thresholding plus recompression can destroy faint strokes (per the same Anthropic compression warning). Save JPEG at quality ≥90.

## Empirical test checklist (on-device, ~30 min)

1. `imagesnap -l` and `ffmpeg -f avfoundation -list_devices true -i ""` — confirm the Link 2 Pro's exact device name/index (and whether a second "virtual" device appears).
2. Snap once from Terminal → approve the TCC camera prompt for Terminal; repeat from Hammerspoon → approve for Hammerspoon.
3. Check native still size from `imagesnap -d "Insta360 Link 2 Pro"` (`sips -g pixelWidth`); if <3840, test `ffmpeg … -video_size 3840x2160` (try `-pixel_format nv12`, then `uyvy422`).
4. In Link Controller: enable DeskView from the meeting position — does the page land in frame? Nudge magnetic mount once if needed; measure what fraction of frame an A4/A5 fills; dial in 2–3× zoom; **save as a preset**; save the meeting framing as another preset.
5. Toggle DeskView off / recall meeting preset ×10 — verify framing repeatability (the undocumented risk); confirm re-enabling AI tracking re-frames correctly as the lazy return path.
